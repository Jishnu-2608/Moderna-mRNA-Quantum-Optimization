## Project Title

Optimization of mRNA Secondary Structure Prediction Using Quantum Computing

## Selected Challenge

WISER Summer Program 2026 – Moderna Challenge

## Background

Messenger RNA (mRNA) plays an important role in modern biotechnology, including the development of mRNA-based medicines and vaccines. The structure formed by an RNA sequence can influence molecular stability, translation efficiency, and overall performance.

RNA molecules fold into different secondary structures depending on interactions between nucleotides:

- Adenine (A)
- Uracil (U)
- Cytosine (C)
- Guanine (G)

Finding the most stable structure requires identifying the structure with the minimum free energy (MFE).

## Problem

For a given RNA sequence, predicting the optimal secondary structure is computationally challenging because the number of possible folding combinations increases rapidly as sequence length increases.

Classical algorithms can efficiently solve short sequences, but larger sequences create scalability challenges.

## Importance

Improving RNA structure prediction can support:

- mRNA medicine design
- Biotechnology research
- Molecular engineering
- Faster computational analysis

Quantum computing and quantum-inspired optimization methods provide a possible approach for solving complex optimization problems.

## Project Goal

This project investigates whether quantum computing techniques can be applied to RNA secondary structure optimization by comparing classical minimum free energy calculations with a quantum optimization approach.

## Expected Outcome

The project aims to:

- Generate RNA sequences
- Analyze classical folding energy
- Implement a quantum computing workflow
- Compare classical and quantum energy results
- Study the potential and limitations of quantum optimization for RNA problems
